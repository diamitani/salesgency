Flagship · built & operated by Patrick Diamitani, GTM AI & Automation Manager, Atlas HXM

AI inbound lead automation — $9.06M in attributed impact.

P

The problem

Atlas HXM's inbound "Contact Us" leads sat untriaged — no instant qualification, no routing, and no way to prove what marketing automation was worth in dollars. Sales-ready buyers waited in line behind job seekers, verification requests, and press inquiries.

B

What was built

An 18-node n8n workflow: every form submission is classified by an AI agent into one of seven intents, the MQL lifecycle stage is set in HubSpot, partnerships route to the right owner, and a grounded reply agent answers sales-ready leads referencing the country and service they asked about. Then a measurement pipeline matched all 166 workflow executions against live CRM deals — a deal counts only if created on or after the contact's workflow run, so pre-existing deals are excluded.

R

Measured outcome

101 MQLs auto-created (72% of runs) · 29 sales deals · $6.38M in attributed pipeline · $130K closed-won · $9.06M total estimated impact. Every dollar traceable to a specific workflow execution ID.

Stack: n8n · Azure OpenAI · Azure AI Search · HubSpot CRM + API · Microsoft Outlook · Claude (agentic analysis) · Python.

More production systems · Atlas HXM

The same operating model, across the GTM org.

AI customer agent

A $250K deal traced to one chat

Problem: site visitors bounced without qualifying; nobody could prove the agent's revenue impact.

Built: a production agent on the public pricing page with versioned guardrails, 4,100+ logged executions, and a weekly attribution stamper writing sourced/influenced tiers onto deals.

Outcome: ~$520K agent-sourced deal value since launch, including a $250K open deal — chat one day, deal the next, traceable to the originating thread ID.

Prospect data pipeline

779 contacts, 0% overlap

Problem: feeding an outsourced SDR agency stale or already-called contacts burns money and brand.

Built: a 9-script Python pipeline merging five enrichment exports, triple-key dedupe against every contact the agency ever called, buying-intent signals scraped from five ATS platforms.

Outcome: 779 unique contacts across 623 companies with confirmed 0% overlap against ~2,900 already-called keys; 91% mobile coverage; tier-ranked 200-contact weekly plans.

AI compliance screening

240 deals screened, zero errors

Problem: deals reached contract stage before anyone checked regulatory eligibility — blockers surfaced at signature time.

Built: a 46-node system mapping messy CRM data to a 30-sector rulebook with a deterministic, unit-tested rules engine issuing the verdict.

Outcome: all ~240 open deals screened; 103 wrongly-stuck deals cleared; ineligible-deal detection doubled; 238/238 production writes, zero errors.

Contract intake

Deal to legal intake in under 3 minutes

Problem: sellers hand-copied deal data into Legal's e-signature form; errors stalled contracting.

Built: seller pastes a deal URL, an agent confirms in 3 steps, and an AI browser agent pre-fills the form in the seller's own logged-in browser — Legal's process untouched, the agent never clicks submit.

Outcome: deal-to-filled-intake in under 3 minutes.

Post-call automation

Four AI agents on every sales call

Problem: reps left calls without follow-ups; country-specific facts were applied inconsistently.

Built: an always-on pipeline triggered after every call — country-aware follow-up email, missed-questions analysis, competitor research, LinkedIn draft, plus an automatic CRM note. Zero rep action.

Outcome: live on every sales call, any device, unattended — verified end-to-end on a real three-country call.

Cost governance

~70% enrichment cost cut by architecture

Problem: runaway enrichment spend — background workflows burned a large credit purchase in about a month, unmonitored.

Built: a standard, cloneable enrichment table — free ICP-fit gate before any paid column, AI columns restricted to top-tier accounts, per-row cost estimates, monthly spend reports to the budget owner.

Outcome: three predictable build profiles (Lean ~15, Balanced ~21, Full ~50 credits/row). Cost control enforced by structure, not by asking users to be careful.
