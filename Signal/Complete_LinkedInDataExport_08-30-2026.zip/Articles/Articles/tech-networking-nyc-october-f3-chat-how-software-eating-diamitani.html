In the startup ecosystem, networking events are considered especially important. In New York City, the men and women who attend these events are known as an elite squad called entrepreneurs. These are their recaps.

On Wednesday, October 19, 2016 I had the opportunity to attend an event relating to the food tech industry in New York City. I thought that the key points were useful and I'd love to share them with you.

But first, here are the details:

  • Event Name: October F3 Chat: How Software is "Eating" the Restaurant Industry
  • Event Organizer(s): Female Founders Fund (F3)
  • Event Date: October 19, 2016
  • Event Time: 6:45pm-9:00pm
  • Event Location: Lifion by ADP   
  • Event Speakers: Krystle Mobayeni, CEO and Cofounder of BentoBox | Somak Chattopadhyay, Managing Partner at Armory Square Ventures
  • Event Host(s): Anu Duggal, Founder of Female Founders Fund (F3)

Great! Now that that's out of the way, let's get down to business. Here are my major takeaways from the panel, hosts and attendees

Lesson 1: On Deciding Whether to Invest in a Founder

When Somak decides who to invest in (notice, it's the entrepreneur being invested in, not necessarily the product or business), the first thing he asks himself is, would I work for them? Would I give up my job and follow them into battle? If yes, then it's a go.

Lesson 2: On the Value of Partnerships

Partnerships are key when it comes to growing a business. The right partnership and affiliate strategy can increase revenue dramatically. When it came to BentoBox, working with another company helped build initial traction and exponential sales.

Lesson 3: On Execution vs Strategy

Rather than spend all your time figuring out what the exact best thing to do is in a hypothetical situation, create a quick plan then execute on it and see what happens. Then measure your results and reiterate until you get to where you need to be.

Lesson 4: On Technical Cofounders

The most difficult hire is the technical cofounder, but when you find one, they can recruit other techies and become a player/coach and vital importance to your team.

Lesson 5: On Joining an Accelerator

Krystle was working part time and freelancing while running her business for a full year before getting any type of capital (although she wanted some). When she joined Techstars, she was a little hesitant at first, because her business had already been operating for a year and had a few original customers.

However, a friend encouraged her to apply and with the right introductions she saw immediately the value of the Techstars Network. Not only did she have direct contact with top investors, she also had the breathing room to settle with her team, work together and get things done.

Lesson 6: On Building a Team

Surround yourself with the right people. Not always on paper, but in heart. You can burn out quickly, but great things happen when you delegate. It's more important that you have people who you can trust that will work hard instead of focusing on any pedigree.

Although Krystle managed to close part of the funding herself, after Techstars, the second round of financing became exponentially easier with a head of sales in tow. Her new partner was not only knowledgeable, he was impactful: it made all the difference to investors to see the chemistry within the team and people who believed in the company.

Lesson 7: On Pricing

For BentoBox, taking a percentage fee of her customer’s product was much more cumbersome on her client than a flat fee or subscription because of all the food tech companies out there taking a piece. Essentially, know your business, know your customer, know your industry and do what’s right.

Lesson 8: On Raising Money

In the fundraising game, sometimes no doesn’t always mean no. However, even though there are a lot of mega funds raising money, there’s a lot of competition for that money. The bar is higher and like dating you can never be sure that someone won’t lead you on or leave you at the alter.

With that being said, the market has slowed down considerably over the last year and the time it takes to raise large growth rounds have increased. It could take months to even a year. You need a strong syndicate of partners to help the company of the raise takes too long.

Lesson 9: On Being Niche by Being Holistic

For BentoBox, their best product quality was the fact that they held a turnkey solution to marketing needs for a hospitality operation. When considering investing, the Armory team decided that while there were plenty of niche players in certain spaces in food tech, this comprehensive approach was actually a niche in itself.

Lesson 10: On Defending Your Positioning

There may be a time when you have to answer an investor question along the lines of: "what's stopping so-and-so from coming in and taking over your market?" The answer to that, or at least for Krystle, was their horizontal positioning. They knew there were competitors, but most of them took vertical positions and were good in some aspects of what BentoBox was providing, but not everything and it wasn't in their business interest or model (at least by looking at current strategy) to move into that space altogether.

There you have it: 10 keys to deepening your understanding of food tech, investing and the professional experiences of founders and investors. Before I go, I want to leave you with a few gems to keep in mind when you're on your own entrepreneurial journey:

Don't be afraid to ask: Do you really need another Michael Jordan motivational quote to tell you to go after what you want?
Don't be afraid to use your network: When you're connected, make use of it. Add value to the people around you and maybe the universe will reward you with value too.
Sometimes you hit a few brick walls. Take a breather: Life gets hard. It's ok to take a break. Embrace your mental help and drive your business, not yourself crazy.
