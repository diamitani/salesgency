What Just Happened in Chicago?

The 2025 Emerging Tech Summit, hosted by 1871, ETHChicago, and Why of AI, brought together innovators across Web3, AI, blockchain, and automation to showcase what’s next.

I attended Day 2 of the summit and a breakout panel which focused entirely on the theme: "AI Conductors – Orchestrating the Future with Intelligent Agents."

This wasn’t a hype fest. It was a serious, candid, technical, and sometimes philosophical conversation about:

  • How AI agents are reshaping work

  • What “implementation” actually means

  • And why trust, testing, and process design are non-negotiable in enterprise AI

Panel Recap: “Orchestrating the Future with Intelligent Agents”

Moderated by Yankun Guo (Partner, Dinsmore), the headline panel brought in voices from:

  • Microsoft

  • Reveal

  • Deloitte

  • Devry University

Key Quotes:

“You need process clarity before automation. AI doesn’t fix chaos—it scales it.”

“Multi-layered guardrails are essential for AI safety, especially in regulated industries.”

“Prompt engineering is the new critical thinking—but prompts without context = noise.”

Breakout Themes & Sessions

The depth of content reflected where AI is actually heading—not just where the VC headlines are.

Sessions Included:

  • Mind Meets Machine — Rise of multimodal & generative AI

  • In AI We Trust — Building enterprise trust and responsible AI

  • Future of Labor & Law — Ethics + automation + employment

  • From Prompt to Product — Using JTBD to architect agent workflows

Implementation Realities

  • Deloitte talked about 2%+ efficiency gains across large delivery chains when AI is used to augment structured processes.

  • Forecasting, data handoffs, and intelligent orchestration were cited as areas where real ROI lives.

  • “Prompt and Pray” was a common pain point—without a clear Job to Be Done, prompt engineering becomes guessing.

  • JTBD (Jobs to Be Done) as the foundational framework for agent design

  • Process implementation before automation — know your system before training your tool

  • AI in Org Design — not just about workflows, but how teams are structured

  • Guardrails in UX/UI — Trust isn’t just backend validation, it’s interface-level design

  • Autonomous coding — what happens when junior devs are replaced by AI?

Process & Architecture

  • AI needs to be layered into workflows - echoed by Microsoft and Reveal.

  • "Don’t scale what you haven’t tested." Guardrails, testing layers, and real-time monitoring were repeated must-haves.

  • AI outputs need contextual verification—especially in legal, finance, and healthcare.

Workforce Impact

  • Agents should augment, not replace. But roles will shift.

  • Developers may evolve into prompt engineers.

  • “If you don’t have juniors, you’ll never get seniors.” Training still matters—even when AI is part of the stack.

  • Best-case scenario? A human conductor using AI tools like a digital orchestra.

Frameworks to Remember

  • JTBD (Jobs to Be Done): Start here before writing a single prompt.

  • SOP Decks: Create standard operating procedures before giving agents tasks.

  • Stacked Guardrails: Think like a security team—multi-layered, continuous monitoring.

Takeaways for Builders and Teams

If you’re building with AI (like we are at GPTPAT and LiveBuildAI), here’s your playbook:

  1. Define the Process → Then Automate It Most orgs skip process design and blame the tool. Get clarity first.

  2. Train People + AI Together Agents don’t replace teams—they extend them. Your best “AI worker” is a well-trained human using great tools.

  3. Use AI to Design AI We saw frameworks like SOPDecks and JTBD embedded directly into AI assistant workflows.

  4. Test for Trust Run agents in beta environments. Use internal audits. Don’t assume alignment—prove it.
