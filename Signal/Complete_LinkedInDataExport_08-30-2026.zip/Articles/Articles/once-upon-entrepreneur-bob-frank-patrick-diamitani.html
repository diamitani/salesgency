Once Upon an Entrepreneur | Bob Frank

Created on 2016-11-01 17:57

Published on 2016-11-01 17:59

Bob Frank is the CEO of Qello, a streaming platform for concert films and music documentaries. Prior to Qello Bob was a senior executive at PolyGram Records as well as a co-founder and President of Koch Entertainment.

Occupation: Entrepreneur

Tell us about your background. Where were you born/raised and what has influenced you towards the path you’re on?

Born and raised in Long Island, NY - have always been on this path but took awhile to realize I was not meant to be a corporate exec. I am now fully in tune with my restless nature as a successful entrepreneur.

When did the moment spark, if at all, where you decided to pursue your path?

I would see a key moment was when a company I was a partner in took advantage of my good nature and cost me alot of money. At that time is when I decided to strike out 100% on my own rather then always have partners. I have always been entrepreneurial but on my own for the last 5 years completely.

When did the moment spark, if at all, where you knew you had chosen the right path?

When I was able to close my first major deal in 2012 - very rewarding moment.

What are the most difficult lessons you’ve learned?

The best deals are the ones you walk away from and also always protect your interests - don't let yourself get diluted into oblivion.

How is dealing with success different than dealing with other experiences?

You have to keep a level head when you win and when you lose - that comes with experience.

If you’re having a bad day, how do you keep yourself up?

At this point I know that tomorrow will most likely be a good day if today is bad - you cannot get frazzled - success and failure in business go hand in hand but success has many parents and failure is an orphan.

What constitutes a good day?

Closing an important deal or beginning the process on an exciting new deal.

Who would you like to have coffee with most and why?

Steve Ross - he was the "master of the game" which was the title of his bio. He's dead so it will be difficult to pull off. He built an empire in my business basically from scratch.

On a scale of 1-10, how important is (a) a team (b) a good idea ( c) a strategy (d) organization?

All 10's but if you have a great team that moves as 1 despite not agreeing all the time it makes the other components more efficient. Nothing worse than a bad team. No team unity = failure in the long run.

If Napoleon Hill was asking you the secret right now, what would you tell him?

Stay positive and persistent! Never give up and be nice to everybody.

How can people contact you, if at all?

bob@bfent.org
