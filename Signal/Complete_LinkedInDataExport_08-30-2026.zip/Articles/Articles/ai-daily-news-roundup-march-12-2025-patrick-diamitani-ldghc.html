Google calls Gemma 3 the most powerful AI model you can run on one GPU  

Google has unveiled Gemma 3, its latest open AI model, which it claims is the most powerful AI system capable of running on a single GPU. Designed for efficiency and accessibility, Gemma 3 aims to democratize advanced AI capabilities by enabling researchers and developers to deploy it on consumer-grade hardware, such as laptops or small servers, without requiring costly cloud infrastructure.

Source: [The Verge]

Elea AI Is Chasing the Healthcare Productivity Opportunity by Targeting Pathology Labs’ Legacy Systems 

Elea AI, a startup focused on modernizing healthcare infrastructure, is tackling inefficiencies in pathology labs by replacing outdated legacy systems with AI-driven solutions. The company aims to streamline workflows in labs, which often rely on manual processes and disjointed software, leading to delays, errors, and operational bottlenecks.

Source: [TechCrunch] 

Google Owns 14% Stake in Anthropic, Holds No Voting Rights, NY Times Reports 

According to a New York Times report cited by TipRanks, Google has acquired a 14% stake in artificial intelligence startup Anthropic but holds no voting rights in the company. The investment, part of a broader $2 billion funding commitment by Google, underscores its strategic push to remain competitive in the rapidly evolving AI sector.

Source: TipRanks News (via The New York Times) 

Salesforce to Invest $1B in Singapore to Boost Adoption of AI 

Salesforce announced plans to invest $1 billion in Singapore over the next five years to accelerate artificial intelligence (AI) adoption across the Asia-Pacific region. The investment, revealed during a regional tech summit, will focus on expanding local data center infrastructure, developing industry-specific AI solutions, and fostering partnerships with Singaporean government agencies and businesses.

Source: [TechCrunch]

Pentesters: Is AI Coming for Your Role? 

The article explores the evolving role of penetration testers (pentesters) in the face of advancing artificial intelligence (AI) tools within cybersecurity. While AI-driven platforms are increasingly capable of automating vulnerability scanning, exploit generation, and attack simulations, the piece argues that these technologies are unlikely to fully replace human pentesters.

Source: [The Hacker News]
