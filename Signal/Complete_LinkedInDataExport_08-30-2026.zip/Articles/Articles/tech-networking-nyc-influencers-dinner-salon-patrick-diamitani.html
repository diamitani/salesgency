Tech & Networking NYC: The Influencer's Dinner (Salon)

Created on 2016-10-27 20:19

Published on 2016-10-27 21:59

In the startup ecosystem, networking events are considered especially important. In New York City, the men and women who attend these events are known as an elite squad called entrepreneurs. These are their recaps.

On Tuesday, October 25, 2016 I had the opportunity to attend The Salon, a private gathering of "exceptional" individuals directly following The Influencer's Dinner. For those who don't know, the Influencer's Dinner is a secret networking experience started in New York in 2009 by behavior expert and social engineer Jon Levy.

Past guests have ranged from TV/movie stars, Olympians, scientists, business owners and executives to famed photographers, comedians, internet celebrities, platinum music artists, Nobel Prize recipients and royalty. I happened to be among the list of invitees simply because I had worked with Jon's sister in the past, and scored an invite that way.

This particular event (and series of events) was spurred on by the launch of Jon's new book, The 2 AM Principle: Discover the Science of Adventure. After traveling the world, being known as a super connector (and one of NY's most eligible bachelors by Gotham Magazine and Elle), he decided to share his experiences with the world.

His E.P.I.C. Model of Adventure is a 4-step process that takes place in every adventure (Establish, Push Boundaries, Increase, Continue) from choosing the right team, the right mission, the right risks and any other adventure in life. Born dyslexic, writing this book was the ultimate achievement. Having a NYT Bestseller would be icing on the cake.

Before I briefly discuss the event, here are the details:

  • Event Name: The Influencer's Dinner (Salon)
  • Event Organizer(s): Jon Levy
  • Event Date: October 25, 2016
  • Event Time: 8:00pm-11:00pm
  • Event Location: Undisclosed

Great! Now that that's out of the way, here are my major takeaways :

In the Beginning

The lower east side penthouse that I arrived at was not the traditional location of the dinners (Jon's Upper West Side apartment). None-the-less, as soon as I entered I was encompassed by the modern white of a three-story palace. My fellow elevator guests were taken aback as it was quite a gorgeous and spacious home, one that you almost don't believe exists in New York City. In front of us was a spiral staircase with an early arrival going down the stairs. Thinking that's where the party was, we followed her.

True to form, there were people talking amongst themselves and I was quickly greeted by a friendly man and his very tall friend. We engaged in small talk, but I noticed something different: there was no urge to "pretend" to be someone you weren't, or to embellish your successes. In fact, no one even talked about what they did. Yet there was this aura around that said,"if you're here, then I trust you." It was my kind of party.

Intermission

After about an hour of "networking" Jon gathered the room to a focal point and began the presentation session of the event. This usually consists of a performer or expert who engages with the audience. This time, since Jon was promoting his book, he decided to use the space to discuss a portion of his book. But not before a comedian from the Daily Show and a renowned magician (one of two, the other the trainer of David Blaine) gave us quite the show. We got laughs, we got magic and we got a special reading.

The After Party

After telling us a great story about a woman name "Elsa" (who he met at the airport and convinced to go on a family vacation to Israel with him), we went back to drinking and meeting new people. Not only did I have a few too many Vodka Selter's (sorry Ravid), I was given thoughtful advice on the way home about a personal issue I had. Talk about a party. I eventually got home (but not to work the next day) and digested the experience.

Final Thoughts

Overall it was great to be around people who didn't care what you did but accepted you no matter what. Although it wasn't a tech event per say, it had the familial community atmosphere of a lot of tech events I've been to. The people were engaging, the entertainment exceptional and refreshments never ending. It was truly E.P.I.C.
