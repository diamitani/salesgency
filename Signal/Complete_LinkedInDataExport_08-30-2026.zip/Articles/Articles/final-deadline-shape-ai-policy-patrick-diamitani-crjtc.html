The U.S. Wants Your Input on Its New AI Action Plan—Here’s What It’s About

The White House’s Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP) and the Networking and Information Technology Research and Development (NITRD) National Coordination Office (NCO) are teaming up to ask for your thoughts on a brand-new Artificial Intelligence (AI) Action Plan. This isn’t just bureaucratic busywork—it’s a big deal, sparked by a Presidential Executive Order signed on January 23, 2025. So, what’s this plan all about, and why should you care? Let’s break it down.

What Is the AI Action Plan?

This plan is a roadmap to keep the United States a global leader in AI. It will outline key policy actions to:

  • Strengthen America’s dominance in AI technology.

  • Support innovation by making sure regulations don’t bog down the private sector—think tech companies, startups, and researchers who are pushing AI forward.

The goal? Maintain the U.S.’s edge in a field that’s transforming everything from healthcare to national security, while avoiding rules that could slow down progress.

Why Is This Important?

AI isn’t just a buzzword—it’s a game-changer. Here’s why this matters to you and the country:

  • Economic Power: AI is fueling growth in industries like medicine (think smarter diagnostics), transportation (hello, self-driving cars), and manufacturing. A solid plan could mean more jobs and a stronger economy.

  • Global Leadership: Being #1 in AI isn’t just bragging rights—it’s about staying ahead of the curve in a tech race with worldwide implications. The U.S. wants to set the pace, not follow.

  • Everyday Life: From smarter apps to safer cities, AI advancements touch your daily routine more than you might think.

But here’s the catch: too much regulation could stifle the companies and innovators driving these breakthroughs. Too little, and we might miss out on managing risks. This plan aims to find the sweet spot—leadership without roadblocks.

Why You Should Get Involved

The government isn’t crafting this in a vacuum. They’re asking everyone—tech experts, business leaders, academics, and yes, even regular folks like you—for input by March 15, 2025. This is your shot to:

  • Shape how AI grows in the U.S.

  • Voice what you think the priorities should be—more innovation? Better safeguards? Both?
