1. GPT-5 drops for all ChatGPT users OpenAI just made the GPT-5 family - Pro, Mini, Nano - available to everyone, including free accounts. It promises sharper reasoning, more consistent outputs, and tighter safety tuning. This is not just an upgrade, it is a flex: OpenAI wants GPT-5 to be the default brain for casual chats and serious builds alike. Read more

2. Medicare’s WISeR pilot brings AI into coverage decisions CMS is launching a six-year trial in six states to use AI to pre-screen prior authorization requests for 17 outpatient procedures. Human clinicians still sign off, but the AI layer is supposed to cut down on low-value care and wasted time. It is efficiency meets ethics — and the privacy hawks are watching closely. Read more

3. Microsoft’s Project Ire hunts malware autonomously Project Ire is an AI agent designed to scan, flag, and classify suspicious files without human babysitting. Early tests: 90% accuracy on what it flags, but only catching 25% of total threats so far. The Defender integration is coming, and when it works at scale, this could be a big win for security teams drowning in alerts. Read more

4. California launches AI upskilling blitz Governor Gavin Newsom is teaming with Google, Microsoft, IBM, and Adobe to offer free AI training across state universities, community colleges, and even high schools. It is a direct play to keep the state’s workforce competitive as AI transforms job markets — and a model other states might want to copy. Read more

5. Apple stays in its privacy lane Tim Cook is betting Apple’s AI future on one thing: trust. The company is leaning hard into on-device processing and minimal data collection. It is slower to roll out and relies on opt-in third-party integrations like ChatGPT, but Apple thinks the long game is about who you trust with your personal data, not who has the flashiest demo.
