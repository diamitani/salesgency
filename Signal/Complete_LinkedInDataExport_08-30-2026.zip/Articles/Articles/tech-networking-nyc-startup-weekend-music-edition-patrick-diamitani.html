Tech & Networking NYC | Startup Weekend NYC: Music Tech Edition

Created on 2016-10-24 18:37

Published on 2016-10-25 14:43

In the startup ecosystem, networking events are considered especially important. In New York City, the men and women who attend these events are known as an elite squad called entrepreneurs. These are their recaps.

From Friday, October 21, 2016 to Sunday, October 23, 2016, I had the opportunity to attend Startup Weekend NYC: Music Tech Edition as a volunteer and prize sponsor. It was one of the greatest tech community building experiences I've ever been a part of.

For those who don't know, Startup Weekend is a global series of events organized by UpGlobal, a Techstars company. They go around the world facilitating concept-to-creation workshops for aspiring and established entrepreneurs.

What does that mean? Basically, in 48 hours, people think of an idea, form teams, validate their concept, validate their business model and create a working prototype. At the end of the weekend, the teams pitch their progress to a panel of judges for bragging rights and a few prizes (such as a free product/explainer video from DreamItReel).

However, the value is not in winning: in fact, there are no losers. Each one of the participants gets the chance to learn the product development process in an innovative and fun way with people that they build life-long friendships with.

This weekend, the participants were given a chance to solve problems and challenges that music creators and fans face. From monetizing their art to bridging the gap between the fan and artist experience to new music curation and recommendation.

In this article I'm going to go through my experience as a volunteer and sponsor of the event and the types of companies/ideas that were formed.

Phew! That was a lot. Now let's get on to what actually happened during the weekend (as told through my eyes).

Opening Night: Friday, October 21 (6pm - 11pm)

Observation 1: A Philanthropic Mission

What I first noticed when I entered the Verizon building at 140 West Street was that there were children in front of me waiting to sign in. I thought it was odd, but then I figured, good for them. What I learned later on was that they were high school students at P-Tech, a specialized technology high school with advancement placement in college curriculum. They were attending the event due to a generous partnership with IBM.

Observation 2: Apprehension in the Air

After I entered the elevator with them and went up to the 2nd floor, I walked over to the sign-in desk in the hallway and introduced myself. My company, DreamItReel, had offered to sponsor a prize package for the winning team. However, I was genuinely interested in music tech so I decided to volunteer and experience the hype. Nonetheless, I could tell that everyone was waiting to see how the weekend would turn out.

Observation 3: Nervous Glee

The apprehension slowly turned giddy as everyone realized that people were showing up and the clock was counting down. The excitement couldn't have been more clear if it was a patronus. However, that didn't stop the nerves from taunting our sanity.

Observation 4: Sign In

Then things got real. The line got longer. The nervousness and apprehension went away and execution became the most important factor. Get these people signed in and start.

Observation 5: Distributed Effort

As things got kicked into high gear, the mastery of each person's individual skill shone through and there was a coordinated teamwork to advance logistics. Orders were given. Orders were followed. The Self went away and the Weekend took over. It had begun.

Observation 6: Hesitation Point

While great things were happening in the team unity department, I could tell as I walked around that the apprehension and nervous glee had set in on the other end of the spectrum. The attendees were trickling in and talking among themselves, but despite the pleasantries, the question on everyone's mind was, "what did I get myself into?"

Observation 7: Clearance of the Air

There came a point when everyone was so engaged in conversation with each other that the persistent nagging in the back of their minds regarding the looming era ebbed and flowed into a friendly attempt to meet their peers and build alliances.

At just the right time, when it seemed as if the anticipation couldn't have been built any more, Ross Palley conjoined the group for an epic game of rock paper scissors (you know the kind where, the winner finds a new opponent and all the losers have to follow the winner around until the final match -- sort of like life). This got everyone in a more hyped up mood as chants of "Teddy, Teddy" filled the air. Teddy, happened to be the guest performer the following day, so it was serendipity that he also won the game.

Observation 8: Presentation

With everyone aligned and gathered in the makeshift auditorium housed in the back of the eloquently weathered co-working space, the moment had arrived. Serial work-shopper Roger Osorio (global facilitator of Startup Weekends with 20+ events to date), not one to let a laugh escape from his audience, launched his presentation by reminiscing on his time as a middle school math teacher at an all girls private school.

He quickly transitioned to his current experience as Entrepreneurship Program leader for IBM, where he brings the Startup Weekend experience to his work every day and got into the presentation; highlighting sponsors, the process and the weekend ahead.

Observation 9: Pitches 

When all was said and done, individuals got a chance to pitch an idea to the group to be voted on as one of the startup ideas to focus on over the weekend. Unable to gauge the interest of participants, it was unclear who, if anyone would go up and share their idea. It was soon very clear that ideas had filled the air and synapses of the crowd and idea after idea was pitched. For a complete overview of all the pitches, follow the twitter.

Observation 10: GTFO

There were so many pitches in fact, that the scheduled segment went an hour past it's allotted time. Unwilling to kick people out and discourage the creative process, the startup weekend team accommodated all ideas and counted all the votes before telling everyone that they didn't have to go home, but they had to get the heck out of there.

Day 2: Saturday, October 22 (8:30am - 10pm)

Observation 1: Team Formation

Everyone had formed a team the night before but a few edits were still being made. People got to know each other and really discuss the project they agreed to work on. Soon, they got the concept or switched teams for a better fit.

Observation 2: Pivots

After some market validation by performing user research, certain teams realized they had to pivot to satisfy their customer base. Those who did knew the importance of survey data when building the first model and gave the people what they wanted.

Observation 3: Team Dynamics

Some teams experienced rifts and had to clear the air before they were able to pull it together. It's strong team cohesiveness that wins, so acknowledging issues helps move the process along. At the end of the day, everything was gravy.

Observation 4: Active Mentorship

Everywhere I looked around the room that day, there were entrepreneurs helping other entrepreneurs. From Coca-Cola to Soundcloud, food startups and record labels, everyone was represented and engaged in helping the teams learn and grow.

Observation 5: Stop, Collaborate and Listen

After soaking in the wisdom of the coaches, the teams gathered themselves and began to delegate and execute. The whole day was focused on getting things done, with groups splitting up and accomplishing their goals. At the end of the night, a special performance was given by ChocoTed: a Chocolate Jazz Experience.

Pitch Night: Sunday, October 23 (5:00pm - 10pm)

When I got there at 4 things were incredibly hectic as everyone was getting prepared for the evening pitches. Sunday night guests who came to take a look at the ideas being presented were already arriving and everyone was focused on the evening's activities.

Finally, the time was right and everyone gathered to hear what Roger had to say. He introduced the sponsors and the judges and then the teams who would be competing. One by one, each team came up and presented a funny, intelligent, insightful and groundbreaking concept and strategy that really made me proud to have met those individuals. Even before food was served everyone knew it would be a hard decision.

The judges came back after an unusually long period of deliberation and Roger had some announcements to make. One of which was that DreamItReel, the video editing platform I work for, was sponsoring a free video for the winners of the competition. Also, all the teams would get a 50% discount and everyone in the audience (and reading this article) would get 25% a professionally edited business product video or explainer.

He also said that for the first time the judges would be providing feedback for all the teams, as they thought they all deserved it. They also were so impressed by the aptitude of the P-Tech kids that they were going to figure out a long term partnership to continue their support in the future. Finally, they announced the winners after much anticipation.

In third place was DJenkins, a chat bot designed to source music recommendations from artificial intelligence, telling you the most popular songs in any data set using machine learning. In 2nd came Rift, a truly transformative technology that allows you (working prototype) to separate sound by it's components, listening to only the percussion in a song or the strings, moving across the vocal structure and beyond.

In first place was Opus: a beautifully designed mobile dashboard for artists and talent bookers. The tool would allow musicians to get gigs and help talent bookers hire acts. They were surprised when they won, jumping up and down and nearly brought to tears. It was a sight to see and experience to be a part of. I was proud of all the teams.

Final Thoughts

Throughout the weekend my time was spent making friends and understanding how entrepreneurship works. It starts with a desire to solve a problem, it's followed by asking questions to those who are affected most by the problem. It transitions to solutions to alleviate those needs. Then it's wrapped with poise, pizazz and productivity to ensure a great experience. It's meshed together and delivered by a team and celebrated as an art, no matter the outcome. The true business of business is people and anything can happen when you put them in a room for 54 hours to talk about music.
