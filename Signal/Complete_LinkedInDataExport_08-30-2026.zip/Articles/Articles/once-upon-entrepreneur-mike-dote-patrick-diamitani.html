Mike Dote is Photographer, videographer and overall content creator. He is the owner of Foxing Productions, a full service production company specializing in building moving picture experiences for all types of clients.

You can see his work at www.foxingproduction.com and www.mikedote.com.

Occupation: CEO/Content Producer

Tell us about our background. Where were you born/raised and what has influenced you towards the path you’re on?

New York, NY. The city moves fast and it has accelerated my life exponentially.

When did the moment spark, if at all, where you decided to pursue your path?

This path's journey was sparked by a series of potential client bids I'd put in for creating still imagery. I lost each bid, even though my rates were industry standard and my work exceptional.

I knew at that point that the industry had developed a cancer that had quickly metasticized. It has grown out of a combination of an oversaturated marketplace and a lack of business education present in those who participate in the still imaging industry.

I knew I needed to plan an exit, so I started to build up a body of motion imagery, and have not regretted it. People need video content more than ever, and the fact that you need to build out an all-star team to do it well is very appealing.

When did the moment spark, if at all, where you knew you had chosen the right path?

The moment I had known things were going right was when one of the videos shot for my company's earliest clients broke 1 million views. That felt really good, and so far we are at 1.7 million.

What are the most difficult lessons you’ve learned?

Top 3:

  1. Everything comes at a cost
  2. Relationships are of the utmost importance
  3. Never ever ever even dare to give up. Progress grows from ongoing effort

How is dealing with success different than dealing with other experiences? 

Success takes time and is pretty hard. Failure doesn't. It comes easily. Takes no effort, and can be reached with a careless moment.

With that said, Learning to fail, is super important. It's an opportunity- to regroup, recompose, and reignite.

Successes and failure alike, are both opportunities, if you have the right mindset.

If you’re having a bad day, how do you keep yourself up?

I chant nam-myoho-renge-kyo

What constitutes a good day?

At the end of the day, I look back and say- I did everything right.

Who would you like to have coffee with most and why?

My father. I always heard what he had said when he was alive, but I rarely 'listened'. He was a damn cool guy!

If Napoleon Hill was asking you the secret right now, what would you tell him? 

There are no secrets, only undiscovered ways.
